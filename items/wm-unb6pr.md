Follow-on from DEV-1395 / PR #5884 (which shipped the v1/v2 filter for the
per-loan at-purchase panels only).

The ~17 IRR / ROI / CDR / CPR panels read gold.realized_cashflows_calendar_month_daily,
which is pre-aggregated per (as_of_date, calendar_month, platform, channel, fund).
A per-loan attribute cannot be filtered at query time once summed, so v1/v2 needs
MODEL_VERSION in that table's GRAIN.

Why it's a real decision, not just work:
- The table is SHARED by 6 platforms (sofi, marlette, prosper, happymoney, upgrade, northpond).
- CDR/CPR/IRR/ROI are NON-additive -> the shared base already synthesises channel='ALL'
  rollup rows; a model_version dimension needs the same 'ALL' logic for every platform.
- IRR/MONTHLY_ROI are computed in Python (canonical fns), so they cannot be recomputed
  in panel SQL as a workaround.
- Needs backfill of all platforms + re-verification of every platform's dashboards for
  one-row-per-(as_of_date,fund) assumptions.

Abhishek's constraint: no standardized schema changes, and no platform-specific gold
tables. Both rule out the easy paths -> needs a call from whoever owns the cashflows
base transform.

Context / prior art:
- DEV-1024 "Add a v2 exp filter" (Kabeer) -> marked Duplicate, folded into DEV-1395.
  Its model_version dashboard variable never worked: nothing ever populated
  positions.model_version for northpond (NULL in the datastore too).
- PR #5884 populated silver.positions.MODEL_VERSION for northpond
  ('northpond_model' = v1/TU, 'northpond_exp_model' = v2/Experian), so the per-loan
  source of truth now exists.
- Current book: 706 v1 / 9 v2 loans; all 9 v2 loans are in the 'experimental' fund.

- 2026-07-20T17:12Z [claude-code] WHAT 'CREDIT GRADE VERSION' ACTUALLY IS (Abhishek 2026-07-20: 'I don't understand it'). MAJOR FINDING: credit_grade_version is 1:1 WITH model_version — it is the SAME v1/v2 split under a different name. Prod evidence, latest snapshot: CGV=1 <-> northpond_model = 706 loans; CGV=2 <-> northpond_exp_model = 9 loans. Zero cross-cells. MECHANISM: both fields are pulled from the SAME winning offers row by the _OFFER_ATTRS_JOIN in northpond/positions.py — TU and EXP offers are UNION ALL'd, each side hardcodes its MODEL_VERSION literal, and each side carries its own CREDIT_GRADE_VERSION from the payload (northpond_tu_offers reads offer.value:creditGradeVersion::INTEGER, northpond_exp_offers reads res.payload:credit_grade_version::INTEGER). TU always emits version 1, EXP always emits version 2, so the two columns are redundant by construction. WHY IT EXISTS / WHAT IT MEANS: CREDIT_GRADE is NOT a letter grade — it is an INTEGER, and the SCALES DIFFER BETWEEN VERSIONS. v1 grades span 3..40 (37 distinct values); v2 grades span 17..83 (8 distinct so far). So grade 30 under v1 is NOT the same thing as grade 30 under v2. The 'version' exists precisely to tell you which scale a grade sits on — it is a guard against pooling or comparing grades across the two grading schemes, NOT a business dimension anyone chose to slice by. HOW IT IS USED ON THE DASHBOARD: exactly one panel, id 597 'Distribution Of Principal Across Credit Grade Version' (piechart, y=37) — SUM(PRINCIPAL_AT_PURCHASE) grouped by CREDIT_GRADE_VERSION off the dead NORTHPOND_AT_PURCHASE_FEATURES table. Since CGV==model_version, that pie is literally 'principal split v1 vs v2' = 706 vs 9 loans, i.e. a ~98/2 split that will always render as one dominant slice. The $credit_grade_version TEMPLATE VARIABLE is used by ZERO panels (confirmed earlier) and also reads the dead table. IMPLICATIONS: (1) panel 597 is arguably redundant with any v1/v2 view and is a weak use of a pie chart; consider dropping it or replacing with a grade-DISTRIBUTION-within-version chart, which is the question the data actually supports. (2) When the v1/v2 workstream resumes, credit_grade_version needs NO separate treatment — filtering model_version filters it identically. (3) The grade-scale divergence (3..40 vs 17..83) is a REAL analytical hazard for any future v1-vs-v2 credit-quality comparison: grades are not comparable across versions and must never be pooled or averaged together.
